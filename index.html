How Sensor Delay Affects Robot Stopping Distance

San Bernardino Science & Engineering Fair

Presenter: Tianrui Li

Abstract

  • Purpose: Investigate how sensor delay, initial motor power, and floor surface affect stopping distance.
  • Method: 120 trials testing 5 delay levels, 4 power levels, and 2 surfaces.
  • Result: Sensor delay had a strong negative correlation (r = -0.81).

1. Introduction & Research Question

Background: Autonomous systems experience latency between sensing and stopping. This delay reduces the safety margin.

Research Question: How do sensor delay, initial motor power, and floor surface affect the final distance between a moving robot and a wall?

2. Hypotheses

  • Sensor Delay: Increasing delay decreases the final distance.
  • Motor Power: Higher power causes the robot to stop closer to the wall.
  • Floor Surface: Different surfaces create different levels of friction.

3. Significance and Materials

This project models real-world collision avoidance.

  • Lafvitech 2WD Programmable Mobile Robot
  • HC-SR04 Obstacle Sensor & Arduino (C++)

5. Results (Correlation)

Pearson correlation between delay and distance: r = -0.810.

This indicates a strong negative correlation.

6. Results (Regression Model)

The model explained 71% of the variation (R-Squared = 0.710).

Y = 20.095 - 0.035(Delay) - 0.960(Power) + 3.443(Surface)

7. Interpretation of Results

  • Sensor Delay (p < 0.05): Statistically significant. Crucial factor for stopping distance.
  • Floor Surface (p < 0.05): Significant. Yoga mat produced ~3.44 cm more distance.
  • Motor Power (p = 0.48): Not statistically significant in this linear model.

8. Extreme Condition: Zero-Distance

At 500 ms delay, many trials resulted in 0 cm.

The system latency was so severe that physical momentum carried the robot into the wall before the brake command executed.

9. Discussion & Conclusion

  • Rapid sensor processing is critical for collision avoidance.
  • Even with accurate sensors, a long response delay neutralizes the safety margin.

10. Limitations & Future Research

  • Limitations: Only one robot tested; zero-distance combined wall contact and stopping failure.
  • Future Improvements: Use high-speed video; implement adaptive braking algorithms based on surface friction.
