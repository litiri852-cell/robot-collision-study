How Sensor Delay Affects Robot Stopping Distance

San Bernardino Science & Engineering Fair

Presenter: Tianrui Li

Abstract

  • Purpose: Investigate how sensor delay, initial motor power, and floor surface affect stopping distance.
  • Method: 120 trials testing 5 delay levels (10-500ms), 4 power levels (25-100%), and 2 surfaces.
  • Result: Sensor delay had a strong negative correlation (r = -0.81).

1. Introduction & Research Question

Background: Autonomous systems experience latency between sensing and stopping. This delay reduces the safety margin.

Research Question: How do sensor delay, initial motor power, and floor surface affect the final distance between a moving robot and a wall?

Hypotheses

  • Sensor Delay: Increasing delay decreases the final distance (the robot moves further during the delay).
  • Motor Power: Higher power causes the robot to stop closer to the wall.
  • Floor Surface: Different surfaces create different levels of friction.

2. Variables

  • Independent Variables:
    • Sensor delay (10, 50, 100, 200, 500 ms)
    • Initial motor power (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%)
    • Floor surface (Wooden floor vs. Yoga mat)
  • Dependent Variable: Final distance from the wall (cm)
  • Controlled: Robot, starting position, obstacle, sensor, measurement method.

3 & 4. Significance and Materials

This project models real-world collision avoidance for autonomous vehicles and warehouse robots.

  • Lafvitech 2WD Programmable Mobile Robot

5 & 6. Experimental Design & Procedure

  • Factorial Design: 5 delays × 4 powers × 2 surfaces × 3 trials = 120 trials.
  • Procedure: Robot activated at a fixed starting line; attempts to stop upon sensing the wall.

7 & 8. Safety & Data Collection

  • Safety: Clear testing area, low power to prevent damage, no human subjects.
  • Data Collection: Measured in cm. Zero distance recorded upon wall contact.

9. Data Analysis Methods

Analyzed using Python (Pandas, Scikit-learn, Statsmodels):

  • Descriptive Statistics
  • Pearson Correlation Analysis
  • Multiple Linear Regression

10. Results (Correlation)

Pearson correlation between sensor delay and distance: r = -0.810.

This indicates a strong negative correlation.

10. Results (Regression Model)

The model explained 71% of the variation (R-Squared = 0.710).

Regression Equation:

Y = 20.095 - 0.035(Delay) - 0.960(Power) + 3.443(Surface)

11. Interpretation of Results

  • Sensor Delay (p < 0.05): Statistically significant. Crucial factor for stopping distance.
  • Floor Surface (p < 0.05): Significant. Yoga mat produced ~3.44 cm more distance.
  • Motor Power (p = 0.48): Not statistically significant in this linear model.

Extreme Condition: Zero-Distance

At 500 ms delay, many trials resulted in 0 cm.

The system latency was so severe that physical momentum carried the robot into the wall before the brake command executed.

12 & 13. Discussion & Conclusion

  • The hypothesis was partially supported.
  • Rapid sensor processing is critical for collision avoidance.
  • Even with accurate sensors, a long response delay neutralizes the safety margin.

14. Limitations

  • Only one robot and two surfaces tested.
  • Motor power was an approximation of speed rather than direct velocity.
  • Zero-distance results combined wall contact and complete stopping failure into one value.

15 & 16. Future Research & Improvements

  • Future Research: Use high-speed video to track braking behavior; develop a logistic regression model for collision probability.
  • Engineering Improvement: Add adaptive braking based on surface; implement a rule to auto-brake if delay exceeds a safety threshold.
