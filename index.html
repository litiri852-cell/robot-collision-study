How Sensor Delay Affects Robot Stopping Distance

San Bernardino Science & Engineering Fair

Presenter: Tianrui Li

1. Abstract

  • Purpose: Investigate how sensor delay, initial motor power, and floor surface affect stopping distance.
  • Method: 120 trials testing 5 delay levels, 4 power levels, and 2 surfaces.
  • Result: Sensor delay had a strong negative correlation (r = -0.81).

2. Introduction & Research Question

Background: Autonomous systems experience latency between sensing and stopping. This delay reduces the safety margin.

Research Question: How do sensor delay, initial motor power, and floor surface affect the final distance between a moving robot and a wall?

3. Hypotheses

  • Sensor Delay: Increasing delay decreases the final distance.
  • Motor Power: Higher power causes the robot to stop closer to the wall.
  • Floor Surface: Different surfaces create different levels of friction.

4. Variables

  • Independent Variables: Sensor delay, motor power, floor surface.
  • Dependent Variable: Final distance from the wall (cm).
  • Controlled: Robot, starting position, obstacle, measurement method.

5. Significance & Materials

This project models real-world collision avoidance.

  • Lafvitech 2WD Programmable Mobile Robot
  • HC-SR04 Obstacle Sensor & Arduino / Python

6. Experimental Design

  • Factorial Design: 120 trials total.
  • Testing Surfaces: Standard wooden floor vs. Yoga mat.

7. Safety & Data Collection

  • Safety: Clear area, low power, no human subjects.
  • Collection: Measured in cm using a fixed reference wall.

10. Data Analysis Methods

Analyzed using Python (Pandas, Scikit-learn):

  • Descriptive Statistics
  • Pearson Correlation Analysis
  • Multiple Linear Regression

11. Results (Correlation)

Pearson correlation: r = -0.810 (Strong negative).

12. Results (Regression Model)

The model explained 71% of the variation (R-Squared = 0.710).

13. Interpretation of Results

  • Sensor Delay (p < 0.05): Statistically significant. Crucial factor.
  • Floor Surface (p < 0.05): Significant. Yoga mat produced ~3.44 cm more distance due to friction.
  • Motor Power (p = 0.48): Not statistically significant in this linear model.

14. Extreme Condition: Collision

At 500 ms delay, many trials resulted in 0 cm.

System latency was so severe that momentum carried the robot into the wall before braking executed.

15. Discussion & Conclusion

  • The hypothesis was partially supported.
  • Rapid sensor processing is critical for collision avoidance.
  • Even with accurate sensors, a long response delay neutralizes the safety margin.

16. Limitations

  • Only one robot and two surfaces tested.
  • Motor power was an approximation of speed.
  • Zero-distance results combined wall contact and complete stopping failure into one value.

17. Future Research

  • Use high-speed video to track braking behavior.
  • Develop a logistic regression model for collision probability.
  • Implement adaptive braking based on surface friction.
